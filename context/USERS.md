## Profile 1: Maya Brooks, Student Who Checks Once a Day

- **Context.** Looks for internships and co-ops between classes and work. Checks postings and messages once a day, usually before heading home. Prince's chapter research found the same habit in a commuting member who missed a practice time because it was shared informally.
- **Job statement.** When I only get one window a day to check postings and messages, I want the openings and deadlines that matter to me to stand out before newer items bury them, so I can apply in time without anyone reminding me.
- **What she does now.** Checks once a day and depends on reminders. The commuting member in the research did the same.
- **Success looks like.** She never learns about a deadline after it has passed.

## Profile 2: Devon Carter, Student Deciding Whether to Apply

- **Context.** Applies for entry-level tech roles. Aaron interviewed a software engineer who applied for a level I role and was offered level II because of an earlier relationship with the recruiter. The engineer had no internship and a couple of strong personal projects, and said projects and experience mattered more than GPA. Aaron also interviewed a tech recruiter, who said a job description describes the employer's preferred candidate, not a strict checklist Not every job is posted on LinkedIn, and alumni one to three years ahead can help a lot.
- **Job statement.** When I search for a tech position, I want to know whether I should apply even if I do not meet every listed requirement, so I can improve my chances without assuming I have to be the employer's ideal candidate.
- **Second job statement.** When I search for a tech position, I want to find openings through sources beyond the major job boards, so I can discover positions I would otherwise miss.
- **What he does now.** Relies on personal projects and existing experience. The engineer learned that GPA mattered less than projects.
- **Success looks like.** He applies with confidence about fit and knows where else to look.

## Profile 3: Jordan Lee, Student Who Cannot Tell If the Effort Is Working

- **Context.** Sends applications and outreach in bursts around classes and work. Ryan's research with an athlete contacting college coaches found the same pattern: personalized emails in bursts, lost track of whom to follow up with, and no way to tell whether she was aiming too high or too low. She judged progress by replies. A parent in Ryan's research declined a camp because there was no sign the school was interested.
- **Job statement.** When applications and outreach pile up on top of classes and work, I want to stay consistent with the people I have already contacted and see whether I am aiming too high or too low, so I can tell whether it is working instead of going quiet for weeks.
- **What she does now.** Sends outreach in bursts and follows up when she remembers. Loses track of who she contacted.
- **Success looks like.** She can say who she contacted, what came back, and whether to adjust.
